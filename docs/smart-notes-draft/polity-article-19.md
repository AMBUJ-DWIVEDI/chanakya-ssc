## 🎭 THE HOOK

Six freedoms, one article, and a number that keeps shrinking. Article 19 originally guaranteed **seven** freedoms in 1950. Today it guarantees **six**. The missing one — the **right to acquire, hold and dispose of property** — was deleted by the **44th Amendment Act, 1978**, and demoted from a Fundamental Right to a mere legal right under Article 300A. That single fact (7 became 6, and property dropped out) is one of the most reliably tested details in SSC Polity, because it sits at the intersection of three favourite exam themes: Fundamental Rights, constitutional amendments, and "what changed and when."

---

## 📖 THE CORE

### The Six (Originally Seven) Freedoms — Article 19(1)
Article 19(1) guarantees all citizens the right to:
1. **(a)** Freedom of **speech and expression**
2. **(b)** Freedom to **assemble peaceably and without arms**
3. **(c)** Freedom to **form associations or unions** (or co-operative societies, added by 97th Amendment, 2011)
4. **(d)** Freedom to **move freely throughout the territory of India**
5. **(e)** Freedom to **reside and settle in any part of the territory of India**
6. **(g)** Freedom to **practise any profession, or to carry on any occupation, trade or business**
- *(Originally also: (f) right to property — DELETED by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978.)*

### Reasonable Restrictions
- None of these freedoms are absolute — Article 19 itself (clauses 2 through 6) allows the State to impose **"reasonable restrictions"** on each freedom, on specific grounds such as: sovereignty and integrity of India, security of the State, public order, decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement to an offence, friendly relations with foreign states.
- This "reasonable restriction" structure is precisely why Article 19 rights are NOT absolute, unlike how they're sometimes colloquially described.

### Key Detail: Citizens Only
- Article 19 rights are available **only to citizens of India**, NOT to foreigners — unlike Article 14 and Article 21, which extend to "any person" (including foreigners) within Indian territory. This citizen-vs-person distinction is a recurring SSC trap.

**"19 is for Citizens, 21 is for Anyone"** — quick rule to separate Article 19 (citizens only) from Article 21 (any person).

## ⚠️ THE TRAP ALERT

- **Article 19 originally listed SEVEN freedoms; only SIX remain today** — the right to property (19(1)(f)) was removed by the 44th Amendment, 1978, and moved to Article 300A as a mere legal (not fundamental) right.
- **Article 19 rights apply only to CITIZENS**, not to all persons — a frequent trap confusing it with Article 14/21, which apply to "any person."
- **None of the six freedoms are absolute** — each is subject to "reasonable restrictions" specified within Article 19 itself; don't treat Article 19 as granting unconditional rights.

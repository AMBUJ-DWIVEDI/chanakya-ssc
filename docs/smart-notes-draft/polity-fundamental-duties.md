## 🎭 THE HOOK

India's original 1950 Constitution gave citizens rights — but never once told them what they owed the country in return. That gap stayed open for 26 years, until the **Emergency era (1975-77)**, when the government decided citizens needed reminding of their obligations too. The **42nd Amendment Act, 1976** — the same amendment that rewrote the Preamble — added an entirely new part to the Constitution: **Part IVA**, with a single Article, **51A**, listing what every citizen is duty-bound to do. The list was drafted on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee**, borrowed in spirit from the **erstwhile USSR's Constitution** (the only major democracy at the time with a comparable duties list), and like the Directive Principles next door in Part IV, made completely **non-justiciable** — no court can punish you for failing to "cherish and follow the noble ideals of the freedom struggle." But unlike a true legal obligation, these duties function more as a constitutional reminder than an enforceable code.

## 📖 THE CORE

### Location & Origin
- **Part IVA, Article 51A** — added by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**, based on the recommendations of the **Swaran Singh Committee**.
- Originally **10 duties**; an **11th duty** was added later by the **86th Amendment Act, 2002** (the same amendment that added Article 21A).
- Inspired by the Fundamental Duties listed in the **Constitution of the USSR**.
- **Non-justiciable** — like DPSPs, not directly enforceable by courts, though Parliament can prescribe penalties for their violation through ordinary law.

### The 11 Fundamental Duties (Article 51A) — Citizens shall:
1. (a) Abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals, institutions, the National Flag, and the National Anthem.
2. (b) Cherish and follow the noble ideals of the freedom struggle.
3. (c) Uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India.
4. (d) Defend the country and render national service when called upon.
5. (e) Promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood among all citizens.
6. (f) Value and preserve the rich heritage of the country's composite culture.
7. (g) Protect and improve the natural environment, including forests, lakes, rivers, and wildlife, and have compassion for living creatures.
8. (h) Develop scientific temper, humanism, and the spirit of inquiry and reform.
9. (i) Safeguard public property and abjure violence.
10. (j) Strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity.
11. **(k)** Provide opportunities for education to one's child or ward between ages 6-14 — added by the **86th Amendment, 2002**.

### Key Facts
- Fundamental Duties apply **only to citizens**, not foreigners (unlike Fundamental Rights, several of which extend to "any person").
- They are based on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee** (1976), constituted by the government specifically to recommend the inclusion of duties.

## 🔑 THE MNEMONIC

**"42 gave 10, 86 made it 11"** — 42nd Amendment (1976) added the original 10 Fundamental Duties; 86th Amendment (2002) added the 11th (child education duty, 51A(k)).

**"Swaran Singh suggested, USSR inspired"** — the committee that recommended them, and the country whose Constitution inspired the idea.

## ⚠️ THE TRAP ALERT

- **There are 11 Fundamental Duties today, not 10** — the 86th Amendment (2002) added the 11th; aspirants who memorized "10 duties" from older material get this wrong.
- **Fundamental Duties apply ONLY to citizens** — unlike several Fundamental Rights (e.g., Articles 14, 21) which extend to "any person," including foreigners.
- **Fundamental Duties are NON-JUSTICIABLE**, just like DPSPs — don't assume they're enforceable like Fundamental Rights; courts cannot directly punish a citizen merely for failing a duty under Article 51A (though Parliament can separately legislate consequences).

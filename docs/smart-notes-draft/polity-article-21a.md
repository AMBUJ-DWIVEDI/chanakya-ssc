## 🎭 THE HOOK

For over 50 years, India's Constitution promised free education only as a *Directive Principle* — a goal for the state to aspire to, not a right any child could walk into court and demand. Article 45 (original 1950 text) told the state to *try* to provide free and compulsory education for children up to age 14 within 10 years. It took until 2002 — more than five decades past that original deadline — for education to be upgraded from an aspiration to a Fundamental Right, when the **86th Constitutional Amendment Act** inserted a brand-new Article, **21A**, directly into Part III. Three years later, Parliament backed it with teeth: the **Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009**, which turned the constitutional promise into an enforceable law with concrete mechanisms — including the now-famous 25% reservation for economically weaker sections in private schools.

---

## 📖 THE CORE

### Text & Origin
- **Article 21A**: *"The State shall provide free and compulsory education to all children of the age of six to fourteen years in such manner as the State may, by law, determine."*
- Inserted by the **86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002** — a SEPARATE, standalone Article, not a sub-clause of Article 21.
- Came into actual force on **1 April 2010**, the date the RTE Act, 2009 was enforced.

### Relationship to Other Provisions
- Before 2002, the Supreme Court had already read a right to education partially into Article 21 itself, in two key cases:
  - **Mohini Jain v. State of Karnataka (1992)** — held right to education flows from right to life (Article 21).
  - **Unni Krishnan v. State of Andhra Pradesh (1993)** — refined this to a right to free education up to age 14, beyond which it is subject to state economic capacity.
- The 86th Amendment also amended **Article 45** (Directive Principle) — it now directs the state to provide early childhood care and education for children BELOW age 6, since 6-14 moved to the Fundamental Right (Article 21A).
- The 86th Amendment additionally added a new **Fundamental Duty** under Article 51A(k) — making it a parent/guardian's duty to provide educational opportunities to their child/ward aged 6-14.

### The RTE Act, 2009
- Operationalizes Article 21A; key provisions include:
  - Free and compulsory education for children aged 6-14 in neighbourhood schools.
  - **25% reservation** in private unaided schools for children from economically weaker/disadvantaged sections.
  - Prohibits screening tests/donations/capitation fees for admission.
  - Prohibits detention/expulsion until completion of elementary education ("no-detention policy," later partially modified).

**"86 touches three places"** — the 86th Amendment touched Article 21A (new right), Article 45 (revised DPSP for under-6s), and Article 51A(k) (new Fundamental Duty) — a 3-for-1 amendment, a favourite "which articles did this amendment affect" trap.

## ⚠️ THE TRAP ALERT

- **Article 21A is a SEPARATE Article from Article 21**, inserted by the 86th Amendment — a very common "is it part of Article 21" conflation trap.
- **The 86th Amendment changed THREE things at once**: added Article 21A, revised Article 45 (now about under-6 early childhood care, not 6-14 education), and added Article 51A(k) (parental Fundamental Duty) — examiners may test any one of the three in isolation.
- **Mohini Jain (1992) and Unni Krishnan (1993) predate the 86th Amendment** — they read education into Article 21 via judicial interpretation BEFORE it became a separate Fundamental Right in 2002; don't assume Article 21A existed earlier than it did.
